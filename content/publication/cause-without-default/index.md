abstract: "Menzies, Hitchcock, Hall, and Halpern have argued that standard causal models must be supplemented with a distinction between default and deviant events. We critically evaluate this proposal. We grant that the notions of 'default' and 'deviant' influence causal judgement, but we claim that this influence is best understood as arising through a general cognitive bias concerning the availability of alternatives."
